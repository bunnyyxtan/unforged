The provenance privacy gap

Provenance needs a trusted signer. Naming the signer can get them jailed.

330journalists imprisoned at the end of 2025 (CPJ prison census)
129journalists and media workers killed in 2025, CPJ's highest annual total

The default today

Public signatures build durable identity and activity graphs. Centralized verifiers see every signer-to-file link. C2PA itself lists privacy, harms and human rights as design goals.

Newsrooms must choose: authentic and traceable, or anonymous and unverifiable.

Unforged removes the choice: the public record proves the file came from an authorized pool and passed a defined workflow, and withholds who approved it.

Sources: CPJ 2025 prison census; CPJ 2026 report on 2025 killings; C2PA 2.3 specification. Links in docs/RESEARCH.md.

Established in Wave 2

  • Exact-file integrity and private pool authority
  • Credential active at the sealing epoch; anonymous k-of-n enrollment
  • Signer and approvers withheld from the record
  • Capture time bounded by ledger time, published to the hour

Not claimed yet

  • Camera-sensor origin (browser capture can be modified)
  • Public-network inclusion: ledger 9 is not on Preprod yet, so the contract runs on the local ledger-9 runtime
  • Network-level anonymity: the relay runs in the browser today
  • Signed C2PA manifests: the assertion is a draft profile

Newsrooms, fact-checkers and human-rights teams that receive media from freelancers, stringers and sensitive sources. Buyer: security, standards or photo-desk leadership.

Moat

Private organizational authorization wired into C2PA, hardware and CMS workflows, plus an anonymity network effect: larger compatible pools protect every contributor better.

3–5 design partners · 50–100 protected contributors · 10,000 real captures · 2 CMS integrations · first paid annual contract. Prices and targets are hypotheses until pilots.
